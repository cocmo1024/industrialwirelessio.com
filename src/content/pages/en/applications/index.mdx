# Applications

Applications are the right starting point for remote telemetry because site context dominates architecture. Asset behavior, terrain, service access, power constraints, and monitoring goals usually shape the winning design before hardware catalogs help.

<div class="signal-strip">
	<div>
		<strong>Use this section for</strong>
		<p>Remote pump stations, water sites, tanks, dispersed utility assets, and other environments where field reality dominates the stack.</p>
	</div>
	<div>
		<strong>What it prevents</strong>
		<p>Choosing radios or gateways too early without understanding access, power, enclosure, and maintenance constraints.</p>
	</div>
	<div>
		<strong>Move next to</strong>
		<p>Network paths once the site profile is stable, then protocols and hardware to refine the footprint.</p>
	</div>
</div>

## Application paths by scenario

Applications are indexed by the operating scenario they serve. Utility telemetry — dispersed, unpowered, rarely visited assets — is this hub's deepest coverage. Process industry paths cover the oil, gas and storage sites where telemetry meets allocation and compliance. Factory-automation coverage (in-plant wireless I/O, AGV and machine retrofit paths) is in production and will join this index as its pages complete review.

## Decision flow

<Steps>
	1. Define the remote asset, site access pattern, and business or operational outcome.
	2. Separate sensing needs from control needs and local buffering needs.
	3. Narrow the likely network path and hardware footprint.
	4. Validate the design against power, enclosure, antenna, and maintenance reality.
</Steps>
