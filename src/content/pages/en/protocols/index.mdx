## How to use this hub

There are three ways into the protocol graph. Use the **spectrum rail** when you know the band you can operate in — licence-free 2.4 GHz, sub-GHz, or a regional allocation — and need to see which protocols live there. Use the **matrix** when you are comparing candidates for a job: topology, update class and power profile decide most real selections before any datasheet is opened. Use the **protocol pages** when you have a candidate and need the verified detail: definition, sourced parameter table, gateway or master capacity, and the engineering notes that say where the protocol stops being the right tool.

## Choosing by scenario

**Process industry — instruments on a plant-wide mesh.** WirelessHART and ISA100.11a were both built for battery-powered field instruments that report process values every few seconds to a minute. They share the same 2.4 GHz radio but are not interoperable; the comparison page works through what actually differs. Start with [WirelessHART](/protocols/wirelesshart/) and the [WirelessHART vs ISA100.11a comparison](/protocols/wirelesshart-vs-isa100/).

**Factory automation — sensors and actuators at machine level.** IO-Link Wireless extends the IO-Link world engineers already use for wired sensors and actuators, with a 5 ms cycle and cable-grade packet error rates over a 10–20 m cell. It is a machine-level link, not a plant network. Start with [IO-Link Wireless](/protocols/io-link-wireless/).

**Spread-out assets — small data, long distance, no plant network.** LoRaWAN trades everything for reach and battery life: tiny payloads, seconds of airtime at the robust end, and regional duty-cycle rules that cap how much any device may send. It fits level, temperature, counting and alarm-state telemetry, and it is the wrong tool for control. Start with [LoRaWAN](/protocols/lorawan/).
